How the arithmetic works

Team implied points = (game total − team spread) ÷ 2

Detroit −5.5 in a 54.5 game gives DET 30.0 and ARI 24.5. A negative spread means the team is favored. A larger team total creates more scoring opportunity; it does not identify the touchdown scorer.

Sportsbooks incorporate information and betting demand, and odds contain a margin. We use the prices as evidence rather than treating them as certainty.

Why props are not precise fantasy projections

A 4.5-reception line is a betting threshold, not a mean of 4.5 catches. Anytime-TD odds measure at least one touchdown, not the expected number. Passing yards alone omit completions, interceptions and rushing. Adding partial markets into a precise projected score would create false confidence.

For scale only: Warren’s 49.5-yard and 5.5-catch thresholds equate to 6.71 points before other statistics. Burden’s 47.5 / 4.5 equate to 6.05; Shakir’s 43.5 / 4.5 to 5.73. These are arithmetic illustrations, not predictions. A real 5-catch, 94-yard game scores 10.02; 4 catches, 25 yards and a TD score 10.00.

How to judge the Law of 10

Use 10 points as a checkpoint and 90 as a baseline team target. Winning depends on the opponent’s total, so that target should not cap upside or force a weak player into a nominal “safe” role.

There is no validated model here for a player’s chance of reaching 10 or your fantasy win probability. Historical hit counts are counts only. Previous precise confidence percentages should not be treated as calibrated.

Three weeks are a small sample. TDs, fumbles, bonuses and returns shift scores sharply. Standings summarize four games; points allowed do not isolate run defense, pass defense or individual coverage.

Exact D/ST tiers and data integrity notes

Points allowed: 0 → +5; 1–6 → +4; 7–13 → +3; 14–17 → +1; 28–34 → −1; 35–45 → −3; 46+ → −5. Yards allowed: <100 → +5; 100–199 → +3; 200–299 → +2; 350–399 → −1; 400–449 → −3; 450–499 → −5; 500–549 → −6; 550+ → −7.
